# 2026 season review

**Status:** draft for the operator, written 2026-09-14 with the regular season and playoff Round 1 complete. Two things are still open and are marked below: the shadow verdict over the last two playoff rounds, and the full-season efficiency of the four teams still in the bracket. Everything else is final.

Six questions, each with its data window and sample size stated before its number, and each ending in a 2027 decision. The reproduction commands are in the last section.

## The one-paragraph version

The bot set lineups better than seven of the nine humans in the league and still finished 9-13, because roster quality, not lineup setting, decided the season. Among the four projection systems, Steamer's rest-of-season line had the best within-day rank skill and the only calibrated hitter bias; the edge over Depth Charts is small but consistent. The 30-day recency blend that replaced year-to-date in June turns out to lose to no blend at all over the full season, so it comes off for 2027. The games-started gate was blind until 2026-08-19 and inert after it. The hourly cadence is 72% no-ops and its cost is a rounding error, so the lever is notification noise, not compute. The whole platform cost about $55 for the season plus a $17 domain, and its biggest operational surprise was that it had no model of the playoffs.

## 1. Did the bot beat the league at lineup setting?

**Window:** the full regular season, weeks 1 to 22 (2026-03-25 to 2026-09-06), all 10 teams, 220 team-weeks. **Source:** Fantrax's own per-period roster snapshots, graded by the same hindsight optimizer the recap site uses (`recap --json --week N`). Efficiency is actual points divided by the hindsight-optimal lineup's points, pooled over the season, not a mean of weekly rates. Round 1 of the playoffs is shown separately because only four teams had a scoring matchup.

| Rank | Team | Efficiency | Actual | Optimal | Record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Houston Swang and Bang | 81.3% | 14,798 | 18,200 | 14-8 |
| 2 | BT95 | 78.1% | 11,551 | 14,784 | 9-13 |
| **3** | **Intentional Balk (rosterbot)** | **76.5%** | **13,107** | **17,144** | **9-13** |
| 4 | jimmydyl | 76.4% | 14,023 | 18,357 | 12-10 |
| 5 | DillonP33 | 74.2% | 11,958 | 16,116 | 7-15 |
| 6 | Voradakis | 73.7% | 12,316 | 16,704 | 11-11 |
| 7 | Pfaadt Wood Kings | 73.2% | 15,912 | 21,732 | 21-1 |
| 8 | Yordan's Schlong | 73.1% | 15,004 | 20,515 | 18-4 |
| 9 | BigZ4 | 71.2% | 10,378 | 14,584 | 3-19 |
| 10 | Finding Nimmo | 69.2% | 12,189 | 17,622 | 6-16 |

League pooled efficiency was 74.7%, the median team 74.0%. The bot finished third at 76.5%, 1.8 points above the median and 4.8 below the leader.

Three things the table says that the rank alone does not:

- **Efficiency and winning are nearly unrelated in this league.** The two teams with the worst efficiency in the top half of the standings, Pfaadt (21-1) and Yordan's (18-4), had the two largest optimal totals. They won on roster depth and left the most on the bench doing it. The bot's 9-13 with the third-best efficiency is the mirror image: a mid-table roster set about as well as it could be.
- **The ceiling is about 81%, not 100%.** Hindsight-optimal credits every bench breakout and every surprise start. Nobody managed a lineup above Houston's 81.3%, and that team is the league's most active manager. The 4,037 points the bot "left on the bench" are mostly unknowable at decision time.
- **The bot's own trend was upward.** From the lineup-gap store (the bot's team only, from 2026-06-16 when grading began): June 72.4% over 15 days, July 76.4%, August 74.6%, September 78.8% through the 13th. Playoff Round 1 was 84.4% (515 of 610), the bot's second-best week of the year, in a 515-605 loss to jimmydyl.

**Decision:** no change to the optimizer. The measurable headroom is the gap to Houston, about 5 points, and closing it means predicting bench breakouts, which is a projection problem, not a slot-assignment one. The lever for 2027 is roster construction, and that is a human decision the trade and waiver tooling informs rather than makes.

## 2. Which projection system should be the 2027 default?

**Window:** the four-system shadow capture, 2026-06-30 to 2026-09-13, 65 to 70 graded days per system and about 1,400 player-days each. **Metric:** within-day Spearman rank correlation between projected and actual points, averaged over days, because per-player-day MAE is nearly blind to what the optimizer needs (it ranks players against each other on one day, it does not forecast their totals). Bias is projected minus actual, in points per game. The capture time moved from 23:40 UTC to 11:30 UTC on 2026-08-21, so the two halves are shown separately and never pooled for the bias figures.

| System | Days | Mean daily ρ | MAE | Hitter bias | Pitcher bias (pre / post 08-21) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **steamer-ros** | 69 | **0.204** | **5.03** | **-0.07** | -2.08 / +1.98 |
| atc-ros | 66 | 0.173 | 5.15 | +0.35 | -1.98 / +1.62 |
| depthcharts-ros (production) | 70 | 0.172 | 5.13 | +0.36 | -2.22 / +1.89 |
| thebatx-ros | 69 | 0.151 | 5.14 | +0.33 | -2.57 / +1.46 |

Paired by day, so the comparison is on identical slates:

| Comparison | Days | Mean Δρ | Better on |
|---|---|---|---|
| steamer vs depthcharts | 68 | +0.026 (sd 0.122) | 40 of 68 (59%) |
| steamer vs atc | 65 | +0.024 (sd 0.121) | 38 of 65 (58%) |
| steamer vs thebatx | 68 | +0.052 (sd 0.133) | 48 of 68 (71%) |
| atc vs depthcharts | 65 | +0.002 (sd 0.111) | 36 of 65 (55%) |

Read honestly: the Steamer edge over Depth Charts is about 1.8 standard errors, suggestive rather than proven on this sample, and the edge over THE BAT X is clear. ATC and Depth Charts are indistinguishable. The bias column is the more decisive finding: three systems over-project hitters by a third of a point per game and Steamer does not, which matters for a blend that weights recent form against the base.

The pitcher bias flip is a measurement artifact worth keeping in mind for 2027: the evening capture graded starting pitchers after their outings were known and under-projected them by 2 to 3 points, the morning capture over-projects them by about the same. Neither half is the right number, and the morning capture is the honest one because it is what the optimizer actually had.

**Decision:** make `steamer-ros` the in-season default for 2027, with the projection preference set per tenant so the operator's team switches first. Keep the four-system shadow running from opening day and re-check at the end of April; if the paired edge has not held over the first four weeks, revert. The pre-season (non-RoS) default moves to `steamer` for the same reason. **Still open:** the last two playoff rounds add about 14 days per system and will be folded in after 2026-09-28.

## 3. Is the 30-day recency blend still earning its keep?

**Window:** the full season, 2026-03-25 to 2026-09-13, 173 days, the bot's own roster, replayed by `backtest --recency-experiment` with `depthcharts-ros` as the base every variant blends on. The June decision (rosterbot-2nd) chose the 30-day window over year-to-date on a six-week sample. This is the same harness over the whole year.

| Strategy (hitters) | Realized points | Mean daily gap |
|---|---|---|
| **base, no blend** | **8,512** | **-18.86** |
| w14 | 8,453 | -19.20 |
| w30 (production) | 8,450 | -19.21 |
| decay21 | 8,394 | -19.54 |
| ytd | 8,338 | -19.86 |

Pitchers were identical across every strategy (5,546 realized, gap -2.51), as they were in June: pitcher slotting is decided by who is starting, not by projection.

Two conclusions. The June finding holds: the 30-day window beat year-to-date by 112 points over the season, close to the one point per day measured then. But blending nothing at all beat the 30-day window by 62 points, about 0.36 per day. The rest-of-season projection already carries the in-season signal, and layering recent form on top of it double-counts it slightly. The effect is small, the direction is consistent across every window length, and the code that produces it is the largest piece of per-run complexity in the blend phase.

**Decision:** turn the hitter recency blend off for 2027 and run on the base projection alone. Keep the code and the harness, and re-run the experiment in mid-May with `steamer-ros` as the base, since that will be the production system and this replay measured Depth Charts. The pitcher blend stays as it is because it costs nothing and changes nothing.

## 4. Did the GS gate help or hurt?

**Window:** 2026-08-19 to 2026-09-13, 26 snapshot days. That is the whole live history: the per-club hybrid forecast that made the gate real shipped on 2026-08-19 (rosterbot-1jvj); before that the flat one-in-five forecast over-projected demand by 4.56 starts a week and the gate never fired. The weekly cap was 12 starts and the floor 10, including through the playoff rounds (verified live for periods 23 to 25).

- Starts suppressed by the gate: **0** in 26 days.
- Probable-starter days on the roster: 46, about 12.4 a week, which sits on the cap.
- Floor alert (the weekly minimum at risk): fired **once**, for period 22, the final regular-season week.
- IL-start alerts (an activated pitcher stranded on the IL): **0**, against a live check that counted real IL and probable-starter inputs every day.

The gate declined nothing because the rotation supplied about as many starts as the cap allowed, so the value-ranked ladder never had to choose. That is not the same as the gate being useless: the roster-shape analysis in the weekly backtest showed the pitcher side at 105 to 110% of cap supply across the same weeks, and a deeper rotation would have crossed it. The only thing the gate did all season that a human would have noticed was the period-22 floor alert.

**Decision:** keep the gate and its constants unchanged; four weeks of a non-binding gate is no evidence for retuning. What changes is the measurement: the weekly `backtest` should print the rotation-supply ratio every week from opening day so the first week the roster crosses 100% is visible before the gate binds, and the floor alert's dedup marker should be checked on 2026-09-28 after its first full playoff cycle.

## 5. Is hourly cadence worth 14 tasks a day?

**Window:** two regimes. Before 2026-09-05 the hourly job ran with `--matchup` and re-decided every remaining day of the week every hour; on 2026-09-05 the split (rosterbot-cem) moved the future-day pass to a once-daily job and left the hourly job today-only. Notifications are from the durable feed, runs from the ledger.

| Regime | Days | Hourly runs / day | Lineup pushes / day | No-op share | Same-day repeat moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hourly `--matchup` (06-17 to 09-04) | 78 | 13 | 12.5 | — | 76% of 5,575 moves |
| today-only (09-05 to 09-13) | 9 | 13.2 | 3.7 | 72% | 22% of 212 moves |

The split removed two-thirds of the pushes and three-quarters of the churn, which was its purpose. What remains: over the last 30 days, 31% of all pushes came from the day's first run at 14:00 UTC and 25% from the evening runs between 00:00 and 03:00 UTC, when West Coast lineups post; 45% of pushes reported a gain under one point, and the median gain was 3.4 points.

Compute is not the argument. Fargate cost the whole platform $1.30 in August for about 900 task launches; halving the hourly job saves cents. The argument is that nearly half of the pushes a human receives are worth less than a point.

**Decision:** keep the hourly cadence and add a minimum-gain threshold to the lineup notification, two points, so the lineup is still applied but the push only goes out when the change is worth reading. That removes roughly 45% of pushes with no change to what the roster does. Cutting the cadence to every two hours between 16:00 and 22:00 UTC is a reasonable second step and should be measured, not assumed, since the evening runs carry a quarter of the value.

## 6. What did it cost to run?

**Window:** the AWS account from the June migration off GitHub Actions through 2026-09-14. Costs are gross, before credits. Ledger records begin 2026-06-17; ops alerting began 2026-07-31.

| Month | AWS total | Of which |
|---|---|---|
| June | $2.14 | CodeBuild $1.57 |
| July | $12.34 | CodeBuild $7.37, S3 $3.73, ECS $0.79 |
| August | $37.83 | domain registration $17.00 (one-off), S3 $10.09, CodeBuild $7.63, ECS $1.30 |
| September to date | $2.37 | S3 $0.64 |

Fargate compute, the thing the 18 schedules actually run on, cost about a dollar a month. CodeBuild, which redeploys on every merge, cost more than the bot itself. S3's August figure is request volume from the hourly state sync, not storage.

Operations, from the run ledger and the alert markers:

- **2,708 runs** recorded between 2026-06-17 and 2026-09-14; **77 failed (2.8%)**, of which 57 were in August, the month FanGraphs put its projections API behind a Cloudflare challenge and Fantrax rotated its client version.
- **Pages to the operator:** 6 task failures, 14 CloudWatch alarms, 1 build-drift detection. The overdue-heartbeat marker fired 22 times, most of them in the first weeks of ops alerting while the schedule table and the heartbeat's tolerances were being tuned to each other.
- **Fantasy notifications:** 1,455 feed records, of which 1,084 lineup pushes, 91 prospect reports, 80 claims recaps, 42 trade reports, 31 waiver lists, 10 GS checks and 117 alerts. **110 of the 117 alerts were stale-cache warnings**, 104 of them in August from the FanGraphs window, one per affected run rather than one per outage.

The incidents that cost real time this season, in order: the weekly-versus-daily period confusion that fired a whole-league false GS alert at the All-Star break (July, rosterbot-uv6 and z3b); the games-started gate being dead code until 2026-08-19 while reporting "0 starts suppressed" every week (rosterbot-1jvj); FanGraphs' Cloudflare challenge from 2026-08-19; and the discovery on 2026-09-14 that the code's season ended on 2026-09-06 while the league's ran to 2026-09-27, which had already produced three separate "post-season" fixes against the wrong boundary (rosterbot-0lyz).

**Decision:** cost is not a lever this year and should not be optimized. Two noise sources are: the stale-cache alert should dedup per source per day (110 alerts to about 20), and the season gate that shipped in rosterbot-0lyz.4 removes the winter's worth of junk runs and pages. The build-drift detector and the heartbeat both earned their keep and stay.

## The 2027 list

In the order they need to land:

1. **Playoff support to completion** (rosterbot-0lyz, slices .6 to .8): the year-end page and the winter schedule switch, once the four open PRs merge.
2. **Default projection system → steamer-ros**, per tenant, with a four-week shadow re-check at the end of April.
3. **Hitter recency blend → off**, base projection only; re-run the replay in mid-May on the new base before locking it.
4. **Lineup notification gain threshold** (2 points) and **stale-cache alert dedup** (one per source per day).
5. **Rotation supply printed weekly** from opening day, so the GS gate's first binding week is visible in advance.
6. **A season-review command**, so the analysis in `docs/season-reviews/2026/analyze.py` runs against the stores directly next September instead of against a scratchpad sync.

## Reproduction

All figures come from these inputs, with the analysis in [docs/season-reviews/2026/analyze.py](2026/analyze.py):

```bash
# Section 1: one JSON per week (the recap site's own grading), regular season + Round 1
for n in $(seq 1 23); do go run . recap --json --week $n > recap/week-$n.json; done

# Section 3: full-season replay of every recency strategy
go run . backtest --recency-experiment --dates 2026-03-25:2026-09-13

# Sections 2, 4, 5, 6: the durable stores, synced locally
aws s3 sync s3://<state-bucket>/analysis/grades/ s3/analysis/grades/
aws s3 sync s3://<state-bucket>/backtest/ s3/backtest/
aws s3 sync s3://<state-bucket>/runledger/ s3/runledger/
aws s3 sync s3://<state-bucket>/notifications/ s3/notifications/
aws s3 sync s3://<state-bucket>/opsalert/ s3/opsalert/
S=. python3 docs/season-reviews/2026/analyze.py

# Section 6: monthly cost by service, gross of credits
aws ce get-cost-and-usage --time-period Start=2026-03-01,End=2026-10-01 --granularity MONTHLY \
  --metrics UnblendedCost --group-by Type=DIMENSION,Key=SERVICE
```
